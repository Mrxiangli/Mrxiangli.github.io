authors: Azam Ikram*, Xiang Li*, Sameh Elnikety, Saurabh Bagchi
Abstract: "Large language models now power a wide range of production services, from interactive assistants to code generation tools. Serving them efficiently requires satisfying three simultaneous and conflicting objectives: low Time-to-First-Token (TTFT) for responsiveness, stable Time-Between-Tokens (TBT) for smooth generation, and high throughput for cost efficiency. Two challenges make this tension difficult to resolve. First, variable-length prefills make safe hybrid batch construction difficult, as admitting a long prefill alongside active decodes can inflate iteration time beyond the TBT budget. Second, decode-only batches leave compute underutilized, yet naively filling this gap with prefill requests risks SLO violations. Existing systems address one challenge at the expense of the other, limiting their overall goodput. We present PaceServe, an LLM serving system that resolves this tension through a two-tier architecture. LP instances maximize throughput by constructing hybrid batches that always include all active decodes and fill remaining compute with prefill requests ordered by Earliest Deadline First (EDF), admitting only those that keep projected iteration time within the TBT budget. When a request's TTFT slack drops below the point where LP can no longer serve it in time, it is offloaded to an HP instance. HP instances dedicate their resources to urgent requests, processing prefills without co-scheduled decodes to minimize interference. We evaluate PaceServe on multiple open-source models, representative datasets, and a real-world arrival trace. PaceServe achieves up to 16% and 18% higher goodput over vLLM and Sarathi-Serve respectively, consistently outperforming all baselines across varying load levels and SLO configurations."
